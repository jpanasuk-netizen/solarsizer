How many solar panels do I need?

The panel-count math in one line, worked examples from a fridge to a full house, and why batteries change the answer.

Quick answer: At 4.5 sun hours, a 1,500 Wh/day refrigerator needs 400 W of panels (one 400 W panel); a 30 kWh/day home needs 7,800 W, or 20 panels at 400 W. Run the calculator below for your exact load.

The math

Every panel-count question reduces to the same division chain — the energy you use per day, spread across the sun hours you actually get, adjusted for real-world losses, divided by what one panel produces:

Number of panels = daily Wh need ÷ sun hours ÷ 0.86 (system derate) ÷ panel watts

The 0.86 is 1 − 0.14: the default 14% total system loss in NREL's PVWatts Version 5 manual (Table 6). In plain terms, it is the energy a typical array loses to dirt on the glass (2%), distant shading (3%), panel-to-panel mismatch (2%), wiring (2%), connections (0.5%), first-sun light-induced degradation (1.5%), panels testing slightly under their label (1%), and downtime (3%). It does not include inverter losses, which PVWatts models separately, or battery charge and discharge losses — see the battery section below. The calculator's sizeSystem uses the same 0.86 and rounds array watts up to the next 100 W.

Battery coupling changes the count

If your panels charge a battery and the battery runs your night loads, the bank adds losses and the array has to refill what the night drains. Two adjustments:

  • Round-trip efficiency: lithium banks deliver ~90–95% of what you charge in; lead-acid ~80–85%. Budget the panel wattage 1.2× over the load-only figure.
  • Autonomy days: a bank sized for 2 days of autonomy still needs to be refilled daily in normal operation — panels sized for one day's load refill a 1-day bank, but a 2-day bank needs roughly the daily load plus whatever was drawn overnight, every day.

Work the other direction with the panels-per-battery recharge math, or size the whole bank first with the battery bank sizer, then size panels to refill it.

Grid-tie vs off-grid changes the goal

Grid-tie panels only need to offset your annual bill — they can overproduce at noon and export. Off-grid panels must cover the worst day of the year, because there's no grid to fall back on. That's why two homes with identical loads can need very different arrays: the off-grid one is sized for December, the grid-tie one for the year.

Sanity checks before you buy

  • Roof space: a 400 W panel is roughly 21 sq ft. Twenty panels ≈ 420 sq ft of usable roof.
  • Inverter headroom: array watts should stay within your inverter's max PV input — see whole-system sizing for pairing.
  • Reduce, then size: every 100 Wh/day you trim (LED swaps, an efficient fridge) removes about 26 W of array at 4.5 sun hours (100 ÷ 4.5 ÷ 0.86). Efficiency is cheaper than panels.
