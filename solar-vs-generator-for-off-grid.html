Solar or generator for off-grid power — which one?

A generator is the cheapest system to buy and the most expensive to own. Solar is the opposite. Ten-year numbers, winter reality, and the hybrid answer.

Quick answer: Solar for daily loads, a small generator for backup. A 1,500 W solar array (~$4,000–6,500 installed) runs a full-time cabin silently for free; a generator covering the same load burns $900–1,500/year in fuel plus maintenance — $11,000–20,000 over 10 years. Solar alone can't clear a two-week cloudy stretch or a well pump surge reliably, so keep a 3,000–4,500 W generator for December and heavy tools.

Where each one fails

Solar fails in northern winters: 1.5–3 sun hours vs summer's 5–6 means the array produces a third to half of nameplate exactly when loads (lighting, longer evenings) rise. Batteries sized for one summer night can't bridge a two-week overcast. A generator fails on the bad day: fuel run out, carb gummed from sitting, or you're not home when the sump needed it. It also can't run unattended — a generator is a chore you perform, solar is infrastructure you own.

The hybrid that actually gets built

Solar sized to summer sun hours plus a battery bank (the solar size calculator and battery bank sizer do the math), then a 3,000–4,500 W generator — sized with GeneratorSizer — charged through an AC-to-battery charger for: the December recharge, well pump startup surge, power tools, and guests' surprises. The generator runs maybe 20–50 hours a year instead of 1,000, cutting its fuel and maintenance bill by 95% while covering solar's one weakness. This is the standard architecture for a reason.

When each pure option is right

Generator-only: seasonal cabins used a few weeks a year (no array to pay off), heavily shaded sites, or mobile setups where panels are a hassle. Solar-only: southern climates with 4.5+ winter sun hours, quiet-obsessed sites, anywhere fuel hauling is brutal. Hybrid: everything north of that — and every full-time homestead.

FAQ

Can solar charge batteries from a generator?

Yes — through a separate AC battery charger or a hybrid inverter's built-in charger. This is the hybrid setup's core loop: the generator charges the bank fast at 100+ A during a winter afternoon, then shuts off; the bank carries the cabin silently overnight and the next day.

What size generator complements a solar cabin?

Big enough to cover the biggest surge load (usually a well pump) plus recharge the bank — a 3,000–4,500 W class for most cabins. Anything larger is wasted hours; anything smaller can't start the pump. Run the surge math in the generator size calculator.
