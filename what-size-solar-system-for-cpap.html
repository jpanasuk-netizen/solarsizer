How to size it

Champion gives watts. Battle Born turns watt-hours into array watts. The missing piece — how long the machine runs — is the example, because neither source publishes a CPAP night length.

  1. Pick a wattage inside the chart: 30–60 W, or 60–120 W heated. 120 ÷ 30 = 4, so the top of the heated range is 4 times the bottom of the unheated range. The array scales by that same factor if the hours stay fixed.
  2. Example night: watts × 8 hours. 30 × 8 = 240 Wh. 60 × 8 = 480 Wh. 120 × 8 = 960 Wh.
  3. Divide by peak sun hours. At 4: 240 ÷ 4 = 60 W, 480 ÷ 4 = 120 W, 960 ÷ 4 = 240 W. Add 20% and 30%: the low machine becomes 72–78 W and the heated high becomes 288–312 W.
  4. Repeat at 5 peak sun hours, and at Phoenix December 4.75 (annual plane-of-array for that run is 6.54, which is the wrong divisor for a winter check).
  5. Battery, from the battery article: watt-hours ÷ 12 V. Two days doubles the amp-hours. A 240 Wh night is 20 Ah for one day; a 960 Wh night is 80 Ah.

About 200 W of solar per 100 Ah is the lithium recharge guideline, not a third CPAP wattage. A one-day bank for the 960 Wh heated case is 80 Ah, so that guideline would point near 200 W of array per 100 Ah of battery — a different question from 960 ÷ 4, and the one the panels-to-charge-a-battery page is for. Size the bank itself on the battery bank sizer.

A Kill A Watt-style meter is the right next measurement: the chart's spread from 30 W to 120 W moves the array from about 72 W to about 312 W at 4 peak sun hours with margin. After you have watt-hours, use the solar size calculator and PVWatts.

FAQ

How many watt-hours is one CPAP night on this page?

The example input is 8 hours. 30 W × 8 = 240 Wh, 60 W × 8 = 480 Wh, and a heated 120 W × 8 = 960 Wh. Champion's ranges are 30–60 W and 60–120 W heated.

What array is that at 4 peak sun hours?

240 ÷ 4 = 60 W, then 72–78 W with 20 to 30 percent. 480 ÷ 4 = 120 W, then 144–156 W. 960 ÷ 4 = 240 W, then 288–312 W.

What battery does the 12 V example imply?

Watt-hours ÷ 12 V is 20 Ah, 40 Ah, or 80 Ah for one day at 240, 480, and 960 Wh. Two days doubles those to 40, 80, and 160 Ah.

Which wattage should I plan around?

The chart runs from 30 W to 120 W. 120 ÷ 30 = 4, so guessing wrong inside the range multiplies the array by as much as 4. Measure your machine and replace both the watts and the example 8 hours.
