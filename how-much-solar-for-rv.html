How many watts of solar do I need for my RV?

Pick a 12 V bank, a daily watt-hour load, and a US city. The array uses this site's recharge formula and the 20-city NREL PVWatts table.

Quick answer: Refilling a 200 Ah 12 V RV bank (2,400 Wh) in one day takes about 430 W of panels in Phoenix, 633 W in Chicago and 737 W in Seattle, using NREL PVWatts peak sun hours of 6.57, 4.46 and 3.83. A fridge using Battle Born's 500 Wh a day example needs only 90 to 154 W.

RV solar picker

Three inputs: bank amp-hours at 12 V, daily watt-hours, and a US city. The city list is the 20-city NREL PVWatts v8 table. Array watts = watt-hours ÷ that city's peak sun hours ÷ 0.85, the same charge-efficiency step as panels to charge a battery. Counts for 100 W, 200 W, and 400 W panels divide those watts by the panel size and round up.

Prefilled with Battle Born's refrigerator example, 100 W × 5 hours = 500 Wh. Replace it with your own daily watt-hours, or set it to 0 to see only the bank refill.

How the watts are derived

Stored watt-hours = amp-hours × volts. Panels to charge a battery states that a 100 Ah 12 V battery holds 1,200 Wh and a 200 Ah 12 V battery holds 2,400 Wh. One-day array watts = those watt-hours ÷ sun hours ÷ 0.85. In assets/app.js, panelsForBattery computes that as wh / sun / 0.85. The 0.85 is that page's charge efficiency for the controller plus battery round-trip. It is not a second copy of PVWatts' system-loss percentage.

Peak sun hours below are the annual average solar radiation on the panel, in kWh/m²/day, from the NREL PVWatts v8 rows: Phoenix 6.57, Chicago 4.46, Seattle 3.83. A darker month has fewer hours than that annual average, so the same watt-hours need more array watts. Shading, tilt, and direction also change the result. Run your own address in PVWatts before buying panels.

  • Phoenix bank: 2,400 ÷ 6.57 ÷ 0.85 = 429.76 W, about 430 W. That is 5 × 100 W, 3 × 200 W, or 2 × 400 W panels.
  • Chicago bank: 2,400 ÷ 4.46 ÷ 0.85 = 633.08 W, about 633 W. That is 7 × 100 W, 4 × 200 W, or 2 × 400 W panels.
  • Seattle bank: 2,400 ÷ 3.83 ÷ 0.85 = 737.21 W, about 737 W. That is 8 × 100 W, 4 × 200 W, or 2 × 400 W panels.
  • Fridge in Phoenix: 500 ÷ 6.57 ÷ 0.85 = 89.53 W, about 90 W. Fridge in Seattle: 500 ÷ 3.83 ÷ 0.85 = 153.59 W, about 154 W.

Counting the 2,400 Wh bank as batteries is on how many solar batteries do I need. AGM and lithium use that capacity differently; the comparison, including the 200 Ah figure from 2,400 Wh ÷ 12 V, is on AGM vs lithium. This page refills the nameplate watt-hours. It does not apply a depth-of-discharge factor.

FAQ

Can an RV air conditioner run on solar?

The sources cited here do not publish a watt figure for an RV air conditioner. Refilling a 200 Ah 12 V bank that holds 2,400 Wh takes about 430 W of panels in Phoenix, 633 W in Chicago and 737 W in Seattle. That is 2,400 ÷ NREL PVWatts peak sun hours of 6.57, 4.46 and 3.83, then ÷ 0.85 from panels to charge a battery.

What if the RV solar array only runs a fridge?

Battle Born's refrigerator example is 100 W × 5 hours = 500 Wh a day. The same formula gives about 90 W in Phoenix (500 ÷ 6.57 ÷ 0.85), about 132 W in Chicago (500 ÷ 4.46 ÷ 0.85) and about 154 W in Seattle (500 ÷ 3.83 ÷ 0.85).

How many watts of solar does a camper van need?

A 100 Ah 12 V bank, the smaller example on panels to charge a battery, holds 1,200 Wh. One-day refill is about 215 W in Phoenix, 317 W in Chicago and 369 W in Seattle (1,200 ÷ 6.57 ÷ 0.85, 1,200 ÷ 4.46 ÷ 0.85 and 1,200 ÷ 3.83 ÷ 0.85).
