How many batteries for 400 watt solar?

Start from the panels and work back to the battery. Pick an array size and a US city; the checker returns the daily harvest and the 12 V amp-hours it refills.

Quick answer: A 400 W array harvests about 2,234 Wh a day in Phoenix, 1,516 Wh in Chicago and 1,302 Wh in Seattle at NREL PVWatts peak sun hours, after this site's 85% charge efficiency. That refills roughly 186 Ah, 126 Ah or 109 Ah of 12 V battery, so one or two 100 Ah batteries.

Derived on this page: watt-hours = 400 × peak sun hours × 0.85, and amp-hours = watt-hours ÷ 12, rounded. Peak sun hours (Phoenix 6.57, Chicago 4.46, Seattle 3.83 kWh/m²/day) are from the NREL PVWatts v8 API, re-run November 17, 2026 with the same settings as the city table. The 0.85 is the charge-efficiency step on panels to charge a battery.

Reading the result

In 19 of the 20 cities, one day's harvest refills more than one 100 Ah battery's nameplate and less than two; Phoenix comes closest to two. Anchorage, at 3.03 peak sun hours, refills about 86 Ah, under one. A bank smaller than one day's harvest fills before the sun goes down, and the rest of the harvest is lost unless a load uses it. A bank larger than the harvest still works, but one average day will not refill it from empty.

The amp-hours here are nameplate amp-hours. This page does not apply a depth-of-discharge factor; how chemistry changes usable capacity is covered on how many solar batteries do I need. The controller that sits between the 400 W array and the battery is sized on how to size a solar charge controller.

Peak sun hours are annual averages. A winter month has fewer hours than the average, so the same 400 W refills less. Shading, tilt, and direction change it too. Run your own address in PVWatts before buying.
