AGM or lithium battery for my off-grid system?

Same amp-hour label, very different batteries. The usable-capacity and cycle-life math that settles which chemistry goes in your bank.

Quick answer: Use the battery manual's depth of discharge and cycle rating. The BB10012i manual rates that model at 100% usable depth of discharge and 3,000–5,000 cycles. The calculator's form uses 80% for its LiFePO4 option and 50% for AGM / lead-acid. Those form values are not printed in that manual.

Where the chemistries differ

The BB10012i manual says the BMS will not charge under 32°F (0°C). A bank that sits full and rarely cycles is not using the cycle rating the same way a daily-cycled bank does. Run both depth-of-discharge options in the battery bank sizer and the solar size calculator on your own load list. The published table for this model is below.

FAQ

Can I mix AGM and lithium in the same bank?

No. The two chemistries use different charge profiles, so one of them charges wrong if they share a controller. Replace whole banks at once, or keep them on separate controllers. The BB10012i manual's absorption range for that model is 14.2–14.6 V.

Do I need a special charge controller or inverter for lithium?

Set the controller to the battery manual. For the BB10012i, absorption is 14.2–14.6 V, float is 13.4–13.8 V, and the maximum charge current is 50 A. A rested lead-acid voltage is a different check, on Trojan's 12 V open-circuit table.

Battle Born's sizing article uses a 2,400 Wh day at 12 V: 2,400 ÷ 12 = 200 Ah, which is two 100 Ah batteries for one day, and two days doubles that to 400 Ah and four batteries. Their article does not divide by a depth of discharge below the 100% this manual publishes.

The solar size calculator defaults are 2 days and 80% depth of discharge. On the same 2,400 Wh, sizeSystem in assets/app.js does 2,400 × 2 ÷ 0.8 = 6,000 Wh, then 6,000 ÷ 12 = 500 Ah. That is five 100 Ah batteries, one more than the article's two-day count. Switch the form to the 50% option and the bank becomes 2,400 × 2 ÷ 0.5 = 9,600 Wh, or 800 Ah at 12 V. The array line stays 2,400 ÷ 4 ÷ 0.86 = 697.67 W, printed as 700 W, at 4 sun hours, because depth of discharge is only in the bank formula. The 50% row is the calculator's AGM / lead-acid option, not a second datasheet.

Using the 80% input on a battery the manufacturer rates at 100% usable is what adds the fifth battery. Charge voltages, the 50 A limit, and the 32°F cutoff are on how to charge LiFePO4 batteries with solar. The full count, including the form's 24 V default, is on how many solar batteries do I need.
