What size solar system for an off-grid cabin?

Panel watts, battery bank, inverter, and charge controller, from daily watt-hours.

Quick answer: Array watts = daily watt-hours ÷ sun hours ÷ 0.86 (NREL PVWatts' default 14% system losses). Run your load list in the solar size calculator.

The formula, once

Everything flows from one number: daily watt-hours. Array watts = daily watt-hours ÷ worst-month sun hours ÷ 0.86 (NREL PVWatts' default 14% system losses: soiling, shading, mismatch, wiring, availability; not inverter or battery losses). Bank watt-hours = daily Wh × days of autonomy ÷ max depth of discharge. LiFePO4's 80% usable depth is why it beats AGM's 50% on paper even before cycle life. Run your own numbers in the solar size calculator.

The refrigerator preset, through the calculator

The refrigerator row in assets/app.js is 150 W × 8 h = 1,200 Wh. At 4 sun hours, 1,200 ÷ 4 ÷ 0.86 = 348.8 W, which sizeSystem prints as 400 W. At 2 days and 80% on a 24 V bank, 1,200 × 2 ÷ 0.8 = 3,000 Wh, and 3,000 ÷ 24 = 125 Ah, which the 10 Ah step prints as 130 Ah. The controller line is 400 ÷ 24 ÷ 0.85 = 19.6 A, listed as 20 A. A cabin day is the sum of the loads you run. This preset is one row, not that sum.

Common sizing mistakes

Heat loads: keep space heat, water heat, and dryers on propane or wood unless you have measured their watt-hours. A heat-pump annual-kWh example is on how many solar panels for a heat pump. Oversized banks, undersized arrays: the array has to replace daily use. Summer-sized arrays in winter: size to worst-month sun hours, or plan generator charging.
