How many solar panels do I need for a cabin?

The count is daily watt-hours ÷ sun hours ÷ 0.86 ÷ the watts of the panel you buy, rounded up.

Quick answer: Square footage does not set the count. The refrigerator preset in assets/app.js is 150 W × 8 h = 1,200 Wh. At 4 sun hours, 1,200 ÷ 4 ÷ 0.86 = 348.8 W, which the calculator prints as 400 W. Panels = that array ÷ the module watts you buy, rounded up. Run the whole cabin list in the solar size calculator.

The math, once

Sum daily watt-hours, then array watts = daily Wh ÷ worst-month sun hours ÷ 0.86. The 0.86 is NREL PVWatts' default 14% system losses. It does not include inverter or battery round-trip losses. The same refrigerator preset, with the bank and controller lines, is on the off-grid cabin solar guide. A flat 5 kWh day is what size solar system for 5 kWh per day.

What changes the count

Adding the refrigerator preset adds 1,200 Wh to the day. A pump's daily watt-hours can be a small part of that day while its startup sets the inverter; the inverter tab uses a 3× motor factor and a 4× compressor factor. Keep electric cooking, water heating, and space heat off the list unless you have measured those watt-hours.

Winter changes the sun-hour input

Type the worst-month sun hours into the calculator, or keep a summer array and add generator charging for the dark months. The battery bank sizer sizes the storage side once the array is settled.

FAQ

Do panel counts change with 200 W vs 400 W panels?

Only the count, never the wattage: 500 W is two 250s or one 500 W module. Larger panels mean fewer roof mounts and less wiring; smaller ones fit irregular roofs and are easier to carry to remote sites.

Can I start with 2 panels and expand later?

Yes, if you buy the charge controller for the final array size upfront — controllers are cheap to oversize, annoying to replace. Batteries also expand modularly with LiFePO4. Inverters are the awkward piece: match the inverter to your biggest future load (usually a pump or tool surge), not today's.

Clearance behind the frame, and the tilt the annual-energy rule uses, are on how to mount solar panels.
